\subsection{Delay Protection with Gradual Weight Equalization}
\label{sec:delay-protection}

WAFT retains the application-specified weighted policy within a configured delay
envelope. When contention pushes delay beyond that envelope, the sender reduces
the total residence budget and gradually reduces weight differentiation. These
two actions serve different purposes: scaling all budgets by the same factor
reduces queueing but preserves the original bandwidth ratios; compressing the
weight ratios moves service toward equal sharing within a common service domain.
Protection changes the budget tracked by the existing inflight controller,
while retaining its observer, window update, and baseline-RTT pacing.

\paragraph{Detecting sustained congestion.}
The operator configures two absolute RTT thresholds, $T_0<\beta<\gamma$,
where $T_0$ is the baseline RTT of the symmetric fabric. The normal-service
boundary $\beta$ reserves headroom below the protection target $\gamma$.
Each sender computes a byte-weighted mean $\bar R_{i,k}$ from usable RTT samples
in a nonoverlapping feedback epoch. Sample weights count the newly confirmed
payload bytes associated with that sample; retransmission-ambiguous samples are
excluded. The epoch length is $2\max(\bar R_{i,k-1},T_0)$, initialized using the
first valid RTT. Thus the budget layer operates more slowly than the inner
half-baseline-RTT window controller.

An epoch mean above $\beta$ is the local criterion for entering protection from
normal operation. A mean at or above $\gamma$ denotes severe congestion and
requires a budget reduction at every positive protection scale. These endpoint observations measure sustained confirmation delay. Without a new valid
sample, the sender holds its protection state; startup and timeout recovery
continue to use the transport's existing mechanisms.

Thresholds are selected with the offline budget: a planned service domain with
concurrent weight $\Omega_{\mathrm{plan}}$ and payload capacity lower bound
$C_{\min}$ should satisfy
$T_0+a\Omega_{\mathrm{plan}}/C_{\min}+h\le\beta$, where $h$ is calibrated
normal-operation delay headroom. The service class then places $\gamma$ within
its protection mean-delay budget, with $\beta<\gamma$. Neither threshold requires
online knowledge of $C_{\min}$ or the active weight sum.

\paragraph{Compressing budgets and weight differences together.}
A service policy declares a positive weight range
$w_{\min}\le w_i\le w_{\max}$. Define $u_i=w_i/w_{\min}\ge1$ and maintain
one protection scale $s_i\in(0,1]$, initially one. The effective target is
\begin{equation}
 q_i(s_i)=a w_{\min}s_i u_i^{s_i},
 \label{eq:protected-budget}
\end{equation}
where $a$ is the nominal budget per unit weight. At $s_i=1$, this target is
exactly $a w_i$. Reducing $s_i$ decreases every target, since
$dq_i/ds_i=a w_{\min}u_i^{s_i}(1+s_i\log u_i)>0$.
The normalization makes this property hold for weights below one as well.
The weight bounds are fixed service-policy configuration.

When flows share the same scale $s$ and effective excess residence, their
budget-tracking rates satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \frac{x_i}{x_j}=\left(\frac{w_i}{w_j}\right)^s.
 \label{eq:protected-share-ratio}
\end{equation}
For example, a nominal weight ratio of four becomes two at $s=1/2$ and
approximately $1.15$ at $s=0.1$. As $s$ approaches zero, relative shares
approach equality while the common budget scale also decreases. In a general
multi-bottleneck network, a common $s$ approaches the unweighted
proportional-fair policy over the applicable routing feasible set.

\paragraph{Updating the protection scale.}
Let $z_{i,k}=\max(0,\bar R_{i,k}/T_0-1)$,
$G=(\gamma-T_0)/T_0$, and $\Delta=(\gamma-\beta)/T_0$.
At each completed feedback epoch, the sender applies
\begin{align}
 p_{i,k}&=\min\left(1,
       \frac{s_{i,k}G}{z_{i,k}+\Delta s_{i,k}}\right),
       \label{eq:protection-proposal}\\
 s_{i,k+1}&=s_{i,k}^{1-\eta}p_{i,k}^{\eta}.
       \label{eq:protection-update}
\end{align}
Here $\eta$ controls the outer budget step. A sufficient gain choice is
$\eta=\min\{1/2,\,1/[1+\log(w_{\max}/w_{\min})]\}$.
The sender holds the resulting target constant during the next epoch.
The layer adds a fixed number of scalars for scale, epoch timing, and RTT
accumulation.

The update is continuous and preserves $0<s_i\le1$. Starting at $s_i=1$,
$\bar R_i>\beta$ makes the scale decrease. More generally, the direction changes
at $\bar R_i=\gamma-(\gamma-\beta)s_i$: delay above this value reduces the
scale, while lower delay restores it. In particular, $\bar R_i\ge\gamma$
always reduces the scale, and $\bar R_i\le\beta$ restores it toward one.
Recovery therefore returns the nominal weighted policy without resetting the
window or changing the allocation signal. At a common constant RTT, all interior scale fixed points also coincide:
Equation~\eqref{eq:protection-proposal} requires $z+\Delta s_i=G$ for every
flow. No sender needs to exchange its scale with another sender.

\paragraph{Equilibrium meaning of the thresholds.}
Consider backlogged flows with a common scale $s$, common baseline and effective
RTT, accurate budget tracking, and a continuously busy service domain of payload
capacity $C$. Their equilibrium satisfies
\begin{equation}
 x_i=C\frac{u_i^s}{\sum_j u_j^s},\qquad
 R=T_0+\frac{a w_{\min}}{C}s\sum_j u_j^s.
 \label{eq:protection-equilibrium}
\end{equation}
If $T_0+a\sum_iw_i/C\le\beta$, the equilibrium has $s=1$ and retains the
original weights. Otherwise, the unique positive protected equilibrium solves
\begin{equation}
 \frac{a w_{\min}}{C}s\sum_j u_j^s+(\gamma-\beta)s
       =\gamma-T_0.
 \label{eq:protection-root}
\end{equation}
The left side is strictly increasing in $s$, is zero at $s=0$, and exceeds
the right side at $s=1$. Hence $0<s<1$ and
\begin{equation}
 R=\gamma-(\gamma-\beta)s\in(\beta,\gamma).
 \label{eq:protection-asymptote}
\end{equation}
Replicating a fixed weight mix increases $R$ monotonically toward $\gamma$
and moves the rate ratios toward one. The total excess stock approaches
$C(\gamma-T_0)$. For a weight range ratio
$U=w_{\max}/w_{\min}$, the same model gives
$x_i/(C/N)\ge U^{-s}$ for every flow among $N$ competitors.
Thus the weakest relative service improves toward the domain's equal share
as differentiation is relaxed.

The thresholds describe the mean-delay equilibrium under the common-service
residence conditions. Each sender maintains its scale independently. Protection is evaluated using delay, utilization,
minimum rate relative to equal sharing, and restoration of nominal weights;
deviation from the original weighted target is an intentional policy change.
