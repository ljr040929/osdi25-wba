\section{Design}
\label{sec:design}
\subsection{Overview}
\label{sec:overview}

WAFT converts application-specified flow weights into excess occupancy targets and tracks them through one aggregate sending window per logical flow. Each sender runs this control loop using its own transmission and acknowledgment events. The excess inflight allocation principle in \S\ref{sec:signal} links the resulting occupancy shares to bandwidth shares across the flow's forwarding paths.

Three components implement the loop. The excess inflight observer measures average excess occupancy over each control period. The weighted window controller adjusts admission to track the occupancy target. The budget manager sets that target from the flow weight and reduces it under sustained congestion.

The window controller runs every \(T_0/2\), where \(T_0\) is the baseline round-trip time. A slower RTT-driven protection loop adjusts the target when contention exceeds the configured operating range. The window controller tracks each new target from its current window, using the same observer and admission mechanism throughout. The underlying transport and load balancer select paths, switch paths, and spray packets.

\subsection{Constant-Space Occupancy Measurement}
\label{sec:observer}

To avoid tracking individual packet ages, the observer reconstructs period-average excess occupancy from aggregate byte counts and send-time moments, using three statistics and a fixed-size history. It performs constant work per transmission event, acknowledgment event, and control-period boundary.

\paragraph{Measuring excess occupancy.}

For flow \(i\), let \(S_i(t)\) and \(A_i(t)\) denote cumulative sent and acknowledged payload bytes. Its actual outstanding occupancy is

\[
I_i(t)=S_i(t)-A_i(t).
\]

As established in \S\ref{sec:signal}, when \(T_0\) is a lower bound on send-to-confirmation time, its excess occupancy is

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:design-9}
E_i(t)=I_i(t)-\big[S_i(t)-S_i(t-T_0)\big].
\end{equation}

The second term is the \emph{baseline inflight amount}: bytes sent within the preceding \(T_0\). Each byte belongs to this baseline portion for \(T_0\) after it is sent. Subtracting the baseline portion from total inflight data gives excess occupancy.

The observer and window controller use fixed periods of length \(T_0/2\). With boundaries \(t_k=kT_0/2\), the controller uses the period average

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:design-10}
\widehat E_{i,k}
=
\frac{2}{T_0}\int_{t_k}^{t_{k+1}}E_i(t)\,dt.
\end{equation}

The baseline age \(T_0\) spans exactly two such periods. This alignment lets the observer recover the average from the current period and two preceding periods.

\paragraph{Computing occupancy from aggregate statistics.}

WAFT maintains three statistics during period \(k\). We omit the flow index when unambiguous. The time accumulation of inflight bytes is

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:design-11}
J_k=\int_{t_k}^{t_{k+1}}I_i(t)\,dt.
\end{equation}

For \(b_s\) bytes sent at time \(s\) in period \(k\), let \(\delta_s=s-t_k\) be its offset from the period start. The other two statistics are

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:design-12}
B_k=\sum_{s\in[t_k,t_{k+1})}b_s,
\qquad
V_k=\sum_{s\in[t_k,t_{k+1})}b_s\delta_s.
\end{equation}

\(B_k\) counts the bytes sent in the period. \(V_k\) sums byte count times send-time offset, retaining how early or late those bytes were sent. Both \(V_k\) and \((T_0/2)B_k\) have units of bytes times time: the latter assigns every byte a full period, while subtracting \(V_k\) leaves the area after each byte's transmission. Thus \((T_0/2)B_k-V_k\) is the baseline inflight area contributed by current-period transmissions.

The baseline inflight area is the baseline inflight amount accumulated over time. For a send of \(b_s\) bytes at time \(s\), its contribution is \(b_s\) times the overlap of \([s,s+T_0)\) with the observation period \([t_k,t_{k+1})\). Because \(T_0\) spans two periods, the overlap has three cases:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Period \(k-2\): keep the beginning.} Bytes sent at \(s=t_{k-2}+\delta_s\) reach age \(T_0\) at \(s+T_0=t_k+\delta_s\). They contribute to the baseline portion for the first \(\delta_s\) time units of period \(k\). Summing their contributions \(b_s\delta_s\) gives \(V_{k-2}\).
\item \textbf{Period \(k-1\): keep the whole period.} These bytes belong to the baseline portion throughout period \(k\). Each contributes \(b_sT_0/2\), giving \((T_0/2)B_{k-1}\).
\item \textbf{Period \(k\): keep the remainder after sending.} Bytes sent at \(s=t_k+\delta_s\) contribute \(b_s(T_0/2-\delta_s)\), giving \((T_0/2)B_k-V_k\).
\end{itemize}

Earlier transmissions have already reached the baseline age when period \(k\) begins. Denote the total baseline inflight area by \(\mathcal Y_k\):

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:design-13}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal Y_k
&=\int_{t_k}^{t_{k+1}}\big[S_i(t)-S_i(t-T_0)\big]\,dt\\
&=V_{k-2}+\frac{T_0}{2}B_{k-1}
  +\left(\frac{T_0}{2}B_k-V_k\right).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Subtracting this computed area from \(J_k\) leaves the excess-byte area; dividing by the fixed period length gives its average.

\begin{lemma}[Exact Period-Average Observation]
\label{lem:main-2}
Let \(T_0\) be a valid lower bound on the confirmation residence of every data unit. With periods of length \(T_0/2\), the mean excess occupancy over period \(k\) is exactly

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:design-14}
\boxed{\widehat E_{i,k}=\frac{J_k-\mathcal Y_k}{T_0/2}.}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof} Integrating Equation~\eqref{eq:design-9} over period \(k\) gives the outstanding-byte area \(J_k\) minus the baseline inflight area \(\mathcal Y_k\) in Equation~\eqref{eq:design-13}. Dividing by \(T_0/2\) yields Equation~\eqref{eq:design-14}.
\end{proof}

The observer computes \(\mathcal Y_k\) from the three periods' aggregate statistics at each boundary, keeping history size independent of packet and path counts.

\paragraph{Event-driven implementation.}

Transport events drive the observer. inflight occupancy is constant between sends and acknowledgments, so the sender accumulates its area incrementally. Before processing an event at time \(t\), it adds \(I_i(t^-)(t-t_{\mathrm{last}})\) to the current area's accumulator and advances \(t_{\mathrm{last}}\). It then updates the outstanding-byte count and transmission statistics as shown in Table~\ref{tab:observer-events}.

\begin{table}[t]
\caption{Observer updates after accumulating the outstanding-byte area.}
\label{tab:observer-events}
\begin{tabularx}{\columnwidth}{@{}p{0.29\columnwidth}X@{}}
\toprule
Event & Observer operation \\
\midrule
Send \(b\) bytes &
Increase \(I_i\) and \(B_k\) by \(b\); increase \(V_k\) by \(b(t-t_k)\). \\
Acknowledge \(b\) bytes & Decrease \(I_i\) by \(b\). \\

\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

The observer uses the transport's byte accounting: each payload byte is counted once at its original send time and released once on acknowledgment. Selective acknowledgments release out-of-order data independently of earlier sequence gaps.

The observer uses six 64-bit fields and four 32-bit fields, totaling 64 bytes of additional state per flow. Each send event, acknowledgment event, and control-period boundary requires \(O(1)\) aggregate operations. This state and processing complexity is independent of the number of paths used by the logical flow.

\subsection{Weighted inflight Window Control}
\label{sec:window}

The window controller turns occupancy error into admission updates, increasing or decreasing each flow's window to track its weighted target.

\paragraph{Mapping application weights to occupancy targets.}

Applications assign each logical flow a positive weight \(w_i\), representing its relative allocation priority. For flows sharing an allocation domain, WAFT configures a common unit occupancy budget \(a>0\) and assigns the nominal target

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:design-15}
\boxed{
q_i=aw_i.
}
\end{equation}

When flows reach these targets, experience a common mean excess residence, and fully utilize the service capacity, the bandwidth-sharing result in \S\ref{sec:signal} gives

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:design-16}
x_i=C\frac{w_i}{\sum_jw_j}.
\end{equation}

Each sender computes its target from its own weight and the configured unit budget. Competition for shared service determines the resulting rates and accounts for the aggregate competing weight.

\paragraph{From an occupancy target to a sending window.}

The sending window \(W_i\) limits admission of unconfirmed payload bytes. Actual outstanding occupancy \(I_i\) counts the admitted bytes awaiting confirmation, and excess occupancy \(E_i\) counts those whose age has reached \(T_0\). These quantities connect the sender's control action to the measured network residence.

At a stable operating point,

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:design-17}
\overline I_i=x_iT_0+\overline E_i.
\end{equation}

At the target, the window accommodates baseline pipeline data \(x_iT_0\) and excess data \(q_i\). WAFT adjusts \(W_i\) using measured excess occupancy \(\widehat E_i\) as feedback.

At each inner-loop period boundary, the sender computes the normalized occupancy error

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:design-18}
e_{i,k}
=
\frac{q_i-\widehat E_{i,k}}
{\max(q_i,\widehat E_{i,k})},
\end{equation}

and updates its aggregate window according to

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:design-19}
\boxed{
W_{i,k+1}
=
\operatorname{clip}_{[L,\,2W_{i,k}]}
\left(
W_{i,k}[1+g e_{i,k}]
\right).
}
\end{equation}

The current configuration uses \(g=0.25\) and a minimum payload window \(L=1000\) bytes.

The occupancy error determines the update direction: occupancy below the target increases the window, and occupancy above the target decreases it. Normalization bounds \(e_{i,k}\) within \([-1,1]\), so the unconstrained multiplicative step lies between \(1-g\) and \(1+g\), or 0.75 and 1.25 with \(g=0.25\). At an interior fixed point with inactive clipping, the update vanishes precisely when

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:design-20}
\widehat E_i=q_i.
\end{equation}

The fixed point therefore satisfies the occupancy target used by the allocation principle.

Each window update governs admission of subsequent data. If the new window falls below actual outstanding occupancy, the sender pauses admission until acknowledgments drain enough data. All packets of a logical flow share this window and observer across their selected paths. Optional pacing based on the baseline RTT schedules the admitted packets. Periodic feedback starts after the first valid confirmation; the underlying transport handles startup and loss recovery.

\paragraph{Adapting to contention.}

Occupancy feedback adapts admission to changes in contention. Additional traffic that increases a flow's residence raises \(\widehat E_i\), producing a negative error and reducing its window. As contention recedes and occupancy falls below the target, the controller expands the window to use the available bandwidth. Each sender makes these adjustments from its local observations.

The following local stability result guides gain selection.

\begin{proposition}[Local Stability under Quasi-Static Common Service]
\label{prop:main-2}
Consider \(n\) continuously backlogged flows sharing a single capacity \(C\) and a common baseline RTT \(T_0\). Suppose each control update observes the exact fluid equilibrium corresponding to the current windows, every window is fully utilized, and clipping is inactive. Let

\[
B=CT_0,\qquad Q=\sum_iq_i.
\]

The positive equilibrium has aggregate window \(S^\star=B+Q\). The linearized window dynamics have eigenvalues

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:design-21}
\lambda_{\mathrm{share}}=1-g,
\qquad
\lambda_{\mathrm{aggregate}}
=1-g\frac{B+Q}{Q}.
\end{equation}

The linearized dynamics are asymptotically stable if and only if

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:design-22}
\boxed{
0<g<\frac{2Q}{B+Q}.
}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch] Let \(S=\sum_iW_i>B\). At the quasi-static fluid equilibrium,

\[
x_i=\frac{CW_i}{S},
\qquad
E_i(W)=W_i\left(1-\frac{B}{S}\right).
\]

The conditions \(E_i=q_i\) yield \(S^\star=B+Q\) and \(W_i^\star=(B+Q)q_i/Q\). Linearizing Equation~\eqref{eq:design-19} around this point separates perturbations of the relative window shares from perturbations of the total window. Their eigenvalues are given in Equation~\eqref{eq:design-21}. Requiring both eigenvalue magnitudes to be below one gives Equation~\eqref{eq:design-22}. Appendix~\ref{app:e-3} derives the full Jacobian.
\end{proof}

At the reference operating point \(Q=B\), the configured gain \(g=0.25\) gives \(\lambda_{\mathrm{share}}=0.75\) and \(\lambda_{\mathrm{aggregate}}=0.5\), placing both modes inside the local stability region of the quasi-static fluid model.

\subsection{Occupancy Budget Configuration}
\label{sec:budget}

The unit budget \(a\) sets the excess occupancy per unit weight; application weights set the desired relative shares. Competing flows in an allocation service domain use the same \(a\), so configuration sets their common occupancy scale while preserving the weight ratios in Equation~\eqref{eq:design-15}.

In a continuously utilized common service domain with capacity \(C\), the relationship derived in \S\ref{sec:signal} gives

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:design-23}
D=\frac{a\Omega}{C},
\qquad
\Omega=\sum_iw_i,
\end{equation}

where \(D\) is the mean excess residence. Hence, increasing \(a\) increases the operating residence level while preserving the intended bandwidth ratios.

WAFT selects \(a\) offline according to the service domain's planned capacity, expected concurrent weights, and latency objective. For a planned active-weight sum \(\Omega_{\mathrm{plan}}\) and reference mean confirmation residence \(R_{\mathrm{ref}}>T_0\), the reference budget is

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:design-24}
\boxed{
a_{\mathrm{ref}}
=
\frac{C(R_{\mathrm{ref}}-T_0)}
{\Omega_{\mathrm{plan}}}.
}
\end{equation}

The default reference is \(R_{\mathrm{ref}}=2T_0\), subject to the latency headroom of the service class. Under this setting, the planned aggregate excess occupancy equals one baseline bandwidth-delay product.

For service domains with several capacity constraints, the planning capacity can be derived from the expected traffic fractions. If \(p_{i\ell}\) is the planned fraction of flow \(i\)'s traffic traversing resource \(\ell\), define

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:design-25}
s_{\mathrm{plan}}
=
\min_\ell
\frac{C_\ell}{\sum_i p_{i\ell}w_i}.
\end{equation}

The capacity-feasible planning vector is \(x_i=s_{\mathrm{plan}}w_i\), yielding

\[
a_{\mathrm{ref}}
=
s_{\mathrm{plan}}(R_{\mathrm{ref}}-T_0).
\]

WAFT uses these capacity and traffic estimates to configure the budget at deployment time.

The budget also sets the resolution of occupancy tracking. Packet granularity, window quantization, and control dynamics produce absolute deviations from the target. For a calibrated bound \(b_i\) on flow \(i\)'s absolute mean-occupancy tracking deviation, a relative deviation target \(\epsilon_q\) requires

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:design-26}
a\ge
\max_i\frac{b_i}{\epsilon_qw_i}.
\end{equation}

Increasing \(a\) reduces the relative effect of absolute tracking deviations and increases excess residence according to Equation~\eqref{eq:design-23}. Allocation precision and latency headroom jointly determine its operating range.

The normal-service RTT boundary \(\beta\) introduced below provides a further configuration constraint. With planned minimum effective capacity \(C_{\min}\), a headroom allowance \(h\), and planned weight \(\Omega_{\mathrm{plan}}\), the normal operating point is selected to satisfy

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:design-27}
T_0+\frac{a\Omega_{\mathrm{plan}}}{C_{\min}}+h
\le\beta.
\end{equation}

This constraint reserves delay headroom for nominal weighted service. The protection loop adapts the budget when sustained contention exceeds this operating range.

\subsection{RTT-Guided Congestion Protection}
\label{sec:protection}

The protection loop addresses the occupancy growth and uneven service that arise during heavy contention. Nominal budgets \(q_i=aw_i\) grow with the active weight sum, so large incasts or sustained overload can push excess residence beyond the planned range. Large weight differences also assign small shares to low-weight transfers. For short low-weight incast transfers, increasing their relative service can accelerate completion and remove transient competitors from the bottleneck.

RTT-guided protection reduces occupancy targets and compresses weight differences as congestion increases. It updates the targets on a slower timescale, and the existing window controller tracks them.

\paragraph{RTT-based congestion assessment.}

WAFT configures two mean RTT thresholds,

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:design-28}
T_0<\beta<\gamma,
\end{equation}

where \(\beta\) sets the normal weighted-service boundary and \(\gamma\) sets the asymptotic mean-RTT objective of protection. Both thresholds are configured time values.

For each protection epoch, the sender computes a byte-weighted mean RTT from valid samples:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:design-29}
\overline R_{i,k}
=
\frac{\sum_j b_jR_j}{\sum_jb_j},
\end{equation}

where \(b_j\) denotes the newly confirmed unique payload bytes represented by sample \(j\). The sender excludes samples whose first-transmission timestamps are ambiguous after retransmission. The epoch duration is

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:design-30}
\tau_{i,k}
=
2\max(\overline R_{i,k-1},T_0),
\end{equation}

initialized using the first valid RTT observation. This loop runs more slowly than the \(T_0/2\) window controller. When an epoch contains no valid samples, the budget scale is held unchanged.

The thresholds define reduction and recovery: RTT above \(\beta\) starts protection at the nominal scale, RTT below \(\beta\) restores compressed budgets, and RTT at or above \(\gamma\) reduces every positive scale. Between the thresholds, the current scale determines the update direction, as derived below.

\paragraph{Joint compression of occupancy and weight differences.}

The service class configures a weight range

\[
0<w_{\min}\le w_i\le w_{\max}.
\]

Each flow maintains a protection scale \(s_i\in(0,1]\), initially \(s_i=1\). Let \(u_i=w_i/w_{\min}\ge1\). WAFT replaces the nominal occupancy target with

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:design-31}
\boxed{
q_i(s_i)
=
aw_{\min}s_i u_i^{s_i}.
}
\end{equation}

Reducing the scale lowers the occupancy target and, across flows with a common scale, narrows the bandwidth ratios.

At \(s_i=1\), it reproduces the nominal target \(q_i=aw_i\). Its derivative is

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:design-32}
\frac{dq_i}{ds_i}
=
aw_{\min}u_i^{s_i}
\big(1+s_i\ln u_i\big)>0.
\end{equation}

Thus, reducing a flow's \(s_i\) lowers its absolute occupancy target. Reducing the participating flows' scales lowers their aggregate budget.

For competing flows with common mean excess residence, a common protection scale \(s\), and accurate occupancy tracking,

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:design-33}
\boxed{
\frac{x_i}{x_j}
=
\frac{q_i(s)}{q_j(s)}
=
\left(\frac{w_i}{w_j}\right)^s.
}
\end{equation}

The exponent \(s\) moves allocation smoothly from the configured weighted shares at \(s=1\) toward equal shares as \(s\) approaches zero. A weight ratio of 4 becomes a bandwidth ratio of 2 at \(s=0.5\) and approximately 1.15 at \(s=0.1\). In Equation~\eqref{eq:design-31}, the common factor \(s\) reduces occupancy, while the exponent compresses the ratios and increases the relative service of low-weight flows.

For \(N\) backlogged flows and \(U=w_{\max}/w_{\min}\), the same common-service model provides

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:design-34}
\frac{x_i}{C/N}
=
\frac{Nu_i^s}{\sum_ju_j^s}
\ge U^{-s}.
\end{equation}

Thus, decreasing \(s\) improves a lower bound on each flow's service relative to equal sharing. This bound quantifies the relative service of low-weight incast transfers sharing capacity with higher-weight flows.

\paragraph{Smooth budget-scale updates.}

Define the normalized excess RTT and threshold parameters

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:design-35}
z_{i,k}
=
\max\left(0,\frac{\overline R_{i,k}}{T_0}-1\right),
\quad
G=\frac{\gamma-T_0}{T_0},
\quad
\Delta=\frac{\gamma-\beta}{T_0}.
\end{equation}

At the end of each valid protection epoch, WAFT computes a candidate scale

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:design-36}
\widetilde s_{i,k}
=
\min\left(
1,\frac{s_{i,k}G}{z_{i,k}+\Delta s_{i,k}}
\right)
\end{equation}

and applies a geometric update

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:design-37}
\boxed{
s_{i,k+1}
=
s_{i,k}^{\,1-\eta}
\widetilde s_{i,k}^{\,\eta}.
}
\end{equation}

Here \(0<\eta\le1\) is the budget-layer adjustment gain. For the configured weight range \(U\), the sufficient gain bound

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:design-38}
0<\eta\le
\min\left\{\frac12,\frac{1}{1+\ln U}\right\}
\end{equation}

also supports the quasi-static local stability result of the budget layer.

The current scale sets the RTT boundary between reduction and recovery. For an interior scale \(s_i<1\), the budget decreases when

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:design-39}
\overline R_i>\gamma-(\gamma-\beta)s_i,
\end{equation}

and increases when the inequality is reversed. At \(s_i=1\), this adjustment begins as soon as the measured mean RTT exceeds \(\beta\). When the mean RTT reaches \(\gamma\), any positive scale is reduced further. As RTT recovers, the budget returns continuously toward its nominal weighted target.

Each outer-loop update installs \(q_i(s_{i,k+1})\), and the inner controller tracks this target from its current window using Equation~\eqref{eq:design-19}. Each sender computes the update from its local scale and RTT statistics.

\paragraph{Protection equilibrium.}

The quasi-static common-service model characterizes the mean RTT reached under sustained overload.

\begin{proposition}[Equilibrium under Sustained Overload]
\label{prop:main-3}
Consider a fully utilized common service domain with capacity \(C\), common baseline RTT \(T_0\), exact occupancy tracking, and a common protection scale \(s\). Let

\[
K(s)=\sum_i u_i^s,
\qquad
R_{\mathrm{nom}}
=
T_0+\frac{a\sum_iw_i}{C}.
\]

If \(R_{\mathrm{nom}}\le\beta\), the nominal scale \(s=1\) is the normal operating equilibrium. If \(R_{\mathrm{nom}}>\beta\), the quasi-static budget model has a unique interior protection equilibrium \(s^\star\in(0,1)\), with

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:design-40}
\boxed{
\beta<R^\star<\gamma.
}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch] Under common residence and accurate occupancy tracking,

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:design-41}
x_i=C\frac{u_i^s}{K(s)},
\qquad
R=T_0+\frac{aw_{\min}}{C}sK(s).
\end{equation}

An interior fixed point of Equation~\eqref{eq:design-37} satisfies

\[
R=\gamma-(\gamma-\beta)s.
\]

Combining the two relations gives

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:design-42}
F(s):=
\frac{aw_{\min}}{C}sK(s)
+(\gamma-\beta)s
=\gamma-T_0.
\end{equation}

Here \(F(0)=0\), and \(R_{\mathrm{nom}}>\beta\) implies \(F(1)>\gamma-T_0\). Moreover,

\[
F'(s)
=
\frac{aw_{\min}}{C}
\sum_i u_i^s(1+s\ln u_i)
+(\gamma-\beta)>0.
\]

Hence a unique solution lies in \((0,1)\). Substituting it into the fixed-point RTT relation establishes \(\beta<R^\star<\gamma\).
\end{proof}

For a fixed weight mix replicated within the quasi-static common-service model, increasing competition drives the equilibrium scale toward zero, the rate ratios toward one, and the equilibrium mean RTT toward \(\gamma\). Appendix~\ref{app:f-4} proves this scaling result.

The protection layer adds a constant number of fields for scale, epoch timing, valid confirmed-byte counts, and RTT accumulation. The same observer and window controller track the resulting targets throughout normal service and overload.
